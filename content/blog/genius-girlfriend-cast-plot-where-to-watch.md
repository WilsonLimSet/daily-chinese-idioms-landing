**Quick answer:** *Genius Girlfriend* (**天才，女友**, *Tiāncái, Nǚyǒu*) is a 2026 Chinese campus-to-adulthood romance starring **Tian Xiwei (田曦薇)** and **Hu Yitian (胡一天)**. It premiered **August 2, 2026** on **iQIYI** — pulled forward from its announced August 5 date — and runs **28 episodes**. Tian Xiwei plays **Lin Zhixia (林知夏)**, a physics prodigy with an **IQ of 174** who is treated as a freak by everyone around her; Hu Yitian plays **Jiang Yubai (江逾白)**, the transfer student who becomes the one classmate willing to sit next to her. They start as academic rivals, are separated when his mother is caught up in a **fraud case**, and reunite as adults — 校服到婚纱, "from school uniform to wedding dress." It's adapted from the Jinjiang novel by **Su Guangtong (素光同)**.

## The plot

Lin Zhixia is a physics genius. That's the whole problem.

She has an IQ of 174 and no instinct whatsoever for other people. She reads as blunt, strange, and unreachable — a **怪胎** (*guàitāi*, "freak"), which is what her classmates actually call her.

At a provincial key high school she ends up deskmates with **Jiang Yubai**, the one person willing to sit there. What follows is not a rescue story. They are **academic rivals first**, meeting through a problem-solving competition and pushing each other at the top of every ranking. The exchange that builds between them is reciprocal and specific: he is a natural socially and teaches her how to be around people; she tutors him. Neither is fixing the other for free.

She wins **保送 (recommended admission) to Fudan University**. Jiang Yubai plans to confess on their graduation trip — and then vanishes, because **his mother becomes caught up in a fraud case**. That's the break the rest of the drama has to repair.

Years later they reunite as adults and work through what the disappearance did to both of them. The story runs all the way into working life and marriage.

## The day-one controversy

The premiere drew immediate criticism, and it wasn't about the writing.

**Hu Yitian is thirty**, and the drama opens with him playing a high-school student. Chinese viewers said so bluntly and at volume — 胡一天太老 ("Hu Yitian is too old") became the headline reaction on day one. It's a recurring complaint in c-drama campus adaptations, where established leads are cast in roles a decade younger than they are, and it tends to shape a show's early word of mouth more than anything on the page.

Worth knowing going in: the reception split early, and the split was about casting rather than story.

## What makes it unusual

Two things.

**First, the heroine's competence is the obstacle, not the prize.** Most campus romances give the female lead a talent that makes her admirable. This one gives her a mind that makes her *isolated* — she is disliked for the thing she's best at, and the drama is honest that being extraordinary in a Chinese classroom is mostly a way of being alone.

**Second, it goes all the way to adulthood.** The story doesn't stop at graduation — 校服到婚纱, "from school uniform to wedding dress," as Chinese coverage puts it. It runs into professional life and marriage, which lets it ask what happens to a prodigy *after* the prodigy years — a question Chinese audiences have real-world reasons to care about. ## The line everyone quotes

Lin Zhixia does not confess like an idol-drama heroine. She confesses like a physicist:

> 我喜欢你，哲学意义上的喜欢和生物学意义上的喜欢。
>
> *"I like you — in the philosophical sense of liking, and the biological sense of liking."*

It's the single most characteristic moment in the drama, and a good test of whether you'll enjoy it.
