**Quick answer:** *Road to Success* (**灿如繁星**, *Càn Rú Fán Xīng*) has a **happy ending** — Chinese recaps called it the most satisfying finale of the year. **Hongjing No. 8 Middle School's football team wins**, going from a disbanded squad of write-offs to champions in a single tournament run (一战成名, "made their name in one battle"). **Lin Wanxing (林晚星) is cleared** — the academic-misconduct accusation that destroyed her career is overturned, and she's free to return to doctoral research. **Wang Fa (王法) lets go** of the guilt he came home carrying, and chooses to *stay* in the small town and keep coaching rather than take his career back overseas. The two commit to each other for the long term. The drama ran **32 episodes** on iQIYI after premiering July 5, 2026.

*Full spoilers for the finale follow.*

## The three questions the ending has to answer

*Road to Success* runs two plots on one track: a sports story and a mystery. By the finale it owes three answers — does the team win, does she get her name back, and does he stop running. It delivers all three, which is why the ending reads as generous rather than neat.

## 1. Does the team win?

**Yes.** Hongjing No. 8 takes the championship.

The satisfying part isn't the trophy — it's what the squad was at the start. This is a **disbanded** team rebuilt out of 问题少年 (*wèntí shàonián*), "problem kids": the ones the school system had already written off, sorted out of the academic track and left to drift. The drama's whole argument is that "problem kid" is a description of circumstances, not of people.

Chinese recaps used **一战成名** (*yī zhàn chéng míng*) — "one battle, and the name is made." It's a phrase from military history, applied here to teenagers on a municipal pitch, and the mismatch is the point.

## 2. Is Lin Wanxing cleared?

**Yes** — and this is the thread that actually matters most.

Lin Wanxing doesn't start the drama as a coach. She starts it as a **psychology PhD who has been falsely accused of academic misconduct (学术不端, *xuéshù bùduān*)** and has lost everything as a result. Her job at Hongjing No. 8 is **sports equipment custodian** — handing out balls and bibs, a position deliberately, humiliatingly beneath her qualifications.

For a Chinese audience this hits harder than it might land in translation. An 学术不端 finding is career-ending and close to unappealable; the accusation alone does the damage, and the accused rarely gets a public correction. Building a feel-good sports drama on top of a false academic-fraud allegation is a genuinely pointed choice.

The finale gives her the correction the real system almost never gives: her academic reputation is restored and the door back to doctoral work reopens. She takes it.

Note what the drama *doesn't* do. She isn't rewarded by getting to stay and coach forever. She gets her actual life back.

## 3. Does Wang Fa stay?

**Yes — and this is the reversal.**

Wang Fa is not a small-town PE teacher. He coached at a **top English football club** and was a world-class name in the profession. He walked away and came home carrying the death of a protégé he blamed himself for, and he did not come back to teach. He came back to **investigate** — which is why he rents the apartment above Lin Wanxing in the first place. The tenancy is surveillance. He suspects she's connected to what happened, and he's hunting the figure behind it, known only by the codename **黑桃Q** (*hēitáo Q*, "Queen of Spades").

So the two leads spend the early drama in a false position: he's watching her, and she doesn't know it.

The finale resolves it by having him **put the case down**. He stops carrying the death as a debt, gives up the idea of returning to the elite career waiting for him, and chooses to keep coaching in the small town. A man who could have any job in world football picks the one with no money and thirty kids in it.

That's the drama's title argument. 灿如繁星 — "brilliant as a sky full of stars" — is about a great many small lights, not one bright one. The ending has its most famous character deliberately become one of the small ones.

## Do Lin Wanxing and Wang Fa end up together?

**Yes.** The finale confirms them as a long-term partnership, with the rest of the young cast dispersing toward their own futures.

The pairing is built as **双向救赎** (*shuāngxiàng jiùshú*), "mutual redemption" — a structure Chinese romance leans on constantly, and one this drama earns better than most, because the two characters are broken in genuinely different ways. She has been robbed of a future she wanted. He is refusing a future he could still have. She needs her name back; he needs permission to stop. Each is the only person positioned to hand the other that.

## The idioms behind the ending

- **狭路相逢** (*xiá lù xiāng féng*) — "to meet on a narrow road," where neither party can step aside. The source novel is titled 《狭路》 for this reason, and the ending is the moment the road finally widens.
- **一战成名** (*yī zhàn chéng míng*) — "one battle, and the name is made." The tournament run.
- **守得云开见月明** (*shǒu dé yún kāi jiàn yuè míng*) — "hold on until the clouds part and the moon comes out." Lin Wanxing's vindication.
- **放下屠刀** (*fàng xià tú dāo*) — "to lay down the butcher's knife," from the Buddhist line about becoming a buddha on the spot. Used loosely for anyone who sets down what they've been carrying. Wang Fa's arc.

## Frequently asked

**Is Road to Success a happy ending?**
Yes. The team wins, Lin Wanxing is exonerated, Wang Fa stays, and the leads end up together.

**Does the football team win the championship?**
Yes — Hongjing No. 8 takes it, from a disbanded squad to champions.

**Does Lin Wanxing go back to academia?**
Her name is cleared and the path back to doctoral work reopens, and she takes it.

**Who is 黑桃Q?**
The codename for the figure behind the death of Wang Fa's protégé — the mystery that brings him home and drives the drama's second plotline.

**Why does Wang Fa rent the apartment above Lin Wanxing?**
To investigate her. He initially suspects she's connected to his protégé's death; becoming her tenant is a pretext.

**How many episodes is it?**
32. Several outlets reported 36 before broadcast, and that figure still circulates.
