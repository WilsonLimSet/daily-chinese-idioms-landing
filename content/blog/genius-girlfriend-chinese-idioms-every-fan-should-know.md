*Genius Girlfriend* (**天才，女友**) runs on two idiom families: the ones about being **exceptional**, and the ones about **growing up next to someone**. The tension between them is the show — because in Chinese, the idioms for brilliance are quietly, consistently about loneliness.

Here are the twelve worth knowing.

---

## The childhood-friends idioms

### [青梅竹马 (qīng méi zhú mǎ)](/blog/qing-mei-zhu-ma)
**"Green plums and bamboo horses."**

The definitive Chinese term for two children who grow up together and later fall in love. From Li Bai's poem 《长干行》: a little girl playing with green plums, a little boy riding a bamboo stick as a hobby-horse.

Lin Zhixia and Jiang Yubai start as high-school deskmates and the drama follows them through university and into working life — 校服到婚纱, "from school uniform to wedding dress." This is the idiom the entire structure is built on, and Chinese viewers will have applied it before episode 2.

### [相濡以沫 (xiāng rú yǐ mò)](/blog/xiang-ru-yi-mo)
**"To moisten each other with spit."**

From Zhuangzi: two fish stranded in a drying rut keep each other alive with their own moisture. It's the idiom for sustaining someone through hardship with whatever little you have — and it's tender precisely because the image is so unglamorous.

The right one for a boy who has nothing to offer the class outcast except sitting next to her.

### [水到渠成 (shuǐ dào qú chéng)](/blog/shui-dao-qu-cheng)
**"When the water arrives, the channel forms."**

Things happen naturally when conditions are ready — no forcing. The shape of a romance built out of years of proximity rather than a single grand gesture.

## The brilliance idioms — and their loneliness

### [出类拔萃 (chū lèi bá cuì)](/blog/chu-lei-ba-cui)
**"To rise above the category, to stand out from the grass."**

From Mencius, describing Confucius. To be conspicuously superior to your peers. The straightforward compliment version of what Lin Zhixia is.

### [鹤立鸡群 (hè lì jī qún)](/blog/he-li-ji-qun)
**"A crane standing among chickens."**

The same idea with the isolation added. A crane among chickens is obviously magnificent — and obviously *not one of them*. Chinese uses this admiringly and uncomfortably at once, which is exactly the register the drama wants: her classmates call her a **怪胎** (freak) for the same quality that makes her a crane.

### [曲高和寡 (qǔ gāo hè guǎ)](/blog/qu-gao-he-gua)
**"The tune is lofty; few can join in."**

The key idiom for this drama, and the saddest one on the list.

From a Warring States story: a singer performs music so refined that almost no one in the crowd can sing along. It means work of such high quality that few can appreciate it — and, by extension, **a person whose level makes company impossible**.

That's Lin Zhixia's actual problem. Not that people dislike her. That there's nobody at her pitch. The whole function of Jiang Yubai is to be the one person who can hold the note.

### [独一无二 (dú yī wú èr)](/blog/du-yi-wu-er)
**"One alone, no second."**

Unique, one of a kind. Note how the phrase is built: the praise and the aloneness are the *same word*, 独 — which also means solitary. Chinese doesn't let you say "unique" without saying "alone."

## The study idioms

### [名列前茅 (míng liè qián máo)](/blog/ming-lie-qian-mao)
**"Name listed among the front rushes."**

To rank at the top. From the reed banners carried at the head of an ancient marching column. The ranking-board idiom — and this is a drama where two people compete for first place for a decade.

### [废寝忘食 (fèi qǐn wàng shí)](/blog/fei-qin-wang-shi)
**"To skip sleep and forget to eat."**

Total absorption in work. Admiring, not concerned — Chinese study culture treats this as a virtue in a way that translation tends to soften.

### [举一反三 (jǔ yī fǎn sān)](/blog/ju-yi-fan-san)
**"Raise one corner, come back with three."**

Confucius, *Analects*: he said he wouldn't keep teaching a student who couldn't be shown one corner of a square and infer the other three. It means grasping a whole principle from a single example — the most precise Chinese description of how a genuinely fast mind works, and the best one-line summary of Lin Zhixia.

### [学富五车 (xué fù wǔ chē)](/blog/xue-fu-wu-che)
**"Learning enough to fill five carts."**

Enormously well-read. The carts are literal: in the Warring States period books were bamboo strips, and five cartloads was a serious library.

### [厚积薄发 (hòu jī bó fā)](/blog/hou-ji-bo-fa)
**"Accumulate thickly, release thinly."**

Su Shi's phrase for building deep reserves and letting them out sparingly. The counter-argument the drama eventually makes to the prodigy myth — what matters isn't the early spike, it's what's stored underneath.
