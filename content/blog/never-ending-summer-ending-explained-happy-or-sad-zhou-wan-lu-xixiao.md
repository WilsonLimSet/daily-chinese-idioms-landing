**Quick answer:** *Never-Ending Summer* (**炽夏**, *Chì Xià*) is a **happy ending — HE, not BE.** Zhou Wan (**周挽**, *Zhōu Wǎn*, Bao Shang'en) and Lu Xixiao (**陆西骁**, *Lù Xīxiāo*, Daniel Zhou) **end up together, engaged, and married in the epilogue.** Episode 28 sees Zhou Wan finally bring down Lu Zhongyue (**陆终岳**); episode 29 resolves the ten-year misunderstanding with a mutual proposal at the abandoned pool — she produces the ring and proposes *first*, he goes down on one knee in reply. Then the drama does something almost no c-drama does: the leads turn to the camera, shout **"炽夏，最后一集"** ("*Never-Ending Summer*, final episode"), and jump off a stack of four school desks. That last beat is what the fandom has been arguing about ever since. The drama ran **29 episodes**, finishing around July 3, 2026 on Mango TV.

*Full spoilers for the finale follow.*

## How the finale actually plays out

The last three episodes run the plot and the romance on separate tracks and land them together.

**Episode 27** is Zhou Wan hunting evidence against **Lu Zhongyue (陆终岳)** — the drama's real antagonist and the reason the workplace-safety investigation exists at all.

**Episode 28** is the payoff: she brings him down. Ten years after a misunderstanding tore the two leads apart, the thing that separated them gets dismantled by her, professionally, on her own terms. That ordering matters — the drama clears the case before it clears the romance, so the reunion isn't a rescue.

**Episode 29** is the reunion. At the abandoned pool, the two finally say the things a decade of silence buried.

## The proposal, and who asks

The scene Chinese viewers universally rated as the finale's best is the **双向求婚** — the mutual proposal.

**Zhou Wan proposes first.** She produces the ring, and it's framed as a **反向求婚** (*fǎn xiàng qiú hūn*), a "reverse proposal" — the woman asking, which is still treated as a notable inversion in the genre. Lu Xixiao then drops to one knee and answers her. Recaps described the atmosphere as **氛围感拉满** — "the mood cranked all the way up."

It's a well-earned scene, and it is thematically exact: this is a couple whose entire problem was that neither would speak first. The ending fixes that by having *both* speak first.

**The epilogue** closes the loop quietly. Zhou Wan stays in safety investigation; Lu Xixiao overhauls the group's safety-production system from the inside; they register their marriage; the grandmother whose illness started the whole story is well.

## The last five minutes: the 双结局 controversy

Then the drama breaks the fourth wall.

After the proposal, the leads face the camera, shout **"炽夏，最后一集"** — announcing the show's own ending — and jump off four stacked classroom desks.

This is the **双结局争议** (*shuāng jié jú zhēng yì*), the "dual-ending controversy," and it split the fandom on three lines:

1. **Emotional whiplash (情绪割裂).** The tonal jump from a tearful, decade-in-the-making proposal to slapstick absurdity in the same episode made it feel to many viewers like two different endings stitched together.
2. **Timing.** A large share of the objection isn't to the *scene* but to its **placement** — most critics said this belonged as a 彩蛋 (post-credits egg), not as the closing beat of the story proper.
3. **Adaptation anxiety.** *Never-Ending Summer* had already drawn a **魔改** (*mó gǎi*, "demon-edit") backlash from readers of the source novel 《坠落》. By the finale, book fans had no stable expectation of what the drama would do, so an unconventional last scene read as one more liberty rather than a flourish.

**Worth being clear about one thing:** despite the "dual ending" name, there is **no confirmed second version**. As of the finale's airing, the production company, director, screenwriter and platform had all stayed silent — no statement, no alternate cut. Every claim about a "real" alternate ending is fan speculation. The name refers to one ending that feels like two, not two endings that exist.

## Is it BE or HE?

**HE.** Unambiguously.

The question gets asked constantly for this one, and the reason is the tone: the drama spends 29 episodes in genuinely bleak territory — illness, money, a calculated approach that becomes real, a decade of separation — and viewers who've been burned by c-dramas that pivot to tragedy in the last hour reasonably brace for it. It doesn't happen. Both leads live, the antagonist falls, they marry.

The novel 《坠落》 also ends happily, so this isn't a case of the adaptation rescuing a tragic source — the 魔改 complaints are about the route, not the destination. We covered that adaptation fight in [Never-Ending Summer & the 魔改 backlash](/blog/never-ending-summer-based-on-novel-zhui-luo-mogai).

## The idioms behind the ending

- **破镜重圆** (*pò jìng chóng yuán*) — "the broken mirror made round again." Separated lovers reunited after ten years; the finale's exact shape.
- **冰释前嫌** (*bīng shì qián xián*) — "old grievances melt like ice." The pool scene, where a decade of assumed betrayal dissolves in one conversation.
- **守得云开见月明** (*shǒu dé yún kāi jiàn yuè míng*) — "hold on until the clouds part and the moon shows." Zhou Wan's whole arc, and the most-quoted line-shape in the finale discourse.
- **苦尽甘来** (*kǔ jìn gān lái*) — "bitterness ends, sweetness comes." The epilogue.

## Frequently asked

**Is Never-Ending Summer BE or HE?**
HE — happy ending. The leads get engaged and married, and the antagonist is brought down.

**Do Zhou Wan and Lu Xixiao end up together?**
Yes. Mutual proposal in episode 29, marriage in the epilogue.

**Is there actually a second/alternate ending?**
No confirmed one. The "dual ending" (双结局) name describes a single finale that swings between two tones — a sincere proposal and a fourth-wall-breaking comic beat. No official alternate cut has ever been confirmed by the production or the platform.

**What happens to Lu Zhongyue?**
Zhou Wan gathers the evidence that brings him down in episode 28.

**How many episodes is it?**
29.

**Does it follow the novel's ending?**
The novel 《坠落》 also ends happily. The adaptation changes a great deal along the way — the 魔改 complaints — but not the fundamental outcome.
