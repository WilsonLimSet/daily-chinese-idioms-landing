**Quick answer:** *Love Beyond the Grave* (**白日提灯**) runs on a supernatural system the drama never stops to explain in full. Here it is. **归墟 (Guīxū)**, the "Return Void," is the realm He Simu rules — a name lifted from genuine Chinese cosmology, not invented for the show. **He Simu (贺思慕)** is not a dead human: she was **born** from the union of the former **Ghost King (鬼王)** and a mortal woman, making her an evil spirit from her first breath and **never once human**. She has ruled for **300 years** and is **400 years old**. Her power runs on a **wish-exchange**: she grants what people want and takes their souls in payment. And the thing that drives the entire romance — she has **no senses of her own**, and experiences colour, warmth, taste and sound only through **Duan Xu (段胥)**, to whom she is bound.

## 归墟 — the Return Void

**归墟** (*guī xū*) is worth knowing because it's real mythology, not set dressing.

- **归 (guī)** — to return, to go back to where one belongs
- **墟 (xū)** — a ruin, a void, an abandoned mound

The name appears in classical Chinese cosmography as a **bottomless gulf into which all waters drain** — every river and every sea pours into it and it never fills. It's the ultimate destination, the place where things that have ended go.

Using that as the name of the ghost realm is a precise choice. Guixu isn't "the underworld" as a punishment-place. It's **where things go when they're finished** — a drain rather than a prison. That framing matters for the ending: when He Simu carries Duan Xu back into Guixu, she isn't taking him to hell. She's taking him where finished things belong.

## He Simu is not a ghost

The most common misreading of the drama, and the one that makes the tragedy land wrong.

**A ghost, in the Chinese tradition, is a dead person.** 鬼 (*guǐ*) is what remains when someone dies badly, or without rites, or with unfinished business. Almost every figure in the *Liaozhai* ghost-romance tradition is a **formerly living** woman. ([The full tradition →](/blog/love-beyond-the-grave-ghost-romance-tradition-liaozhai))

He Simu is not that. She was **born** — the child of the previous **Ghost King (鬼王)** and a mortal woman. She has never been alive in the human sense and therefore has never died. She is an **evil spirit by birth**, not by circumstance.

This is why she can't follow anyone. A dead lover could, in principle, be reunited with a dead beloved. She's outside that entirely: she doesn't die, so she can't go where the humans go.

## The wish-exchange

Her operation is an economy, and a very old-fashioned one: **she grants wishes and takes souls.**

This is squarely in the Chinese supernatural-bargain tradition, where spirits are transactional rather than merely malevolent — you get what you asked for, and the price is exact and not negotiable. The horror is never that the spirit cheats. It's that it doesn't.

For a ruler of the dead, it's also a self-portrait: she deals in other people's desires because she has no equipment for her own.

## The senses — the device the whole drama runs on

Here is the mechanism that makes this more than a standard ghost romance.

**He Simu has no senses.** No colour, no warmth, no taste, no sound as a living body registers them. She rules a realm and commands the dead, and she cannot feel a summer afternoon.

Bound to **Duan Xu**, she experiences the world **through his five senses (五感)**.

Everything follows from that:

- Her ability to feel anything at all is **borrowed**, and the lender is a mortal man in a war.
- Every sensory pleasure the drama shows her — and it lingers on them — is on loan.
- The romance and the tragedy are **the same mechanism**. There is no version where she keeps the senses and loses him.

Which is why the canonical ending does what it does: Duan Xu dies, and the world she had finally learned to feel **goes dark again**. Not metaphorically. Mechanically. ([Both endings explained →](/blog/love-beyond-the-grave-dual-ending-explained))

## The 22 graves

The single most important number in the drama.

Across four centuries, He Simu has loved — or attempted to love — **twenty-two human beings**. Every one of them died. Not because she killed them. Because **humans die**, and she doesn't.

Twenty-two graves is the drama's argument in one image: immortality as an accumulating grief rather than a prize. Duan Xu is not her first. He's the twenty-third, and she already knows exactly how it ends.

That's what makes her performance in the early episodes — the fragile war orphan, wincing at blood, pleading for protection — read as something other than manipulation. She has done this before, many times, and she knows the shape of it. ([Character deep-dive →](/blog/love-beyond-the-grave-character-study-he-simu-duan-xu-idioms))

## Duan Xu's side

He isn't a passive donor of senses.

**Duan Xu (段胥)** is a young general of **Great Liang** — and, unusually, a **literary scholar** who remade himself into a military commander, with one obsession: recovering the lost northern provinces.

So both leads are self-made against type. She's a ruler who can't feel; he's a scholar who chose war. Neither is doing the thing their nature suggests.

*(The drama's former title, **慕胥辞**, is built from their names — **慕** from 思**慕**, **胥** from 段**胥**, with 辞 meaning an ode or a parting.)*

## The idioms this world runs on

- **[天长地久](/blog/tian-chang-di-jiu)** (*tiān cháng dì jiǔ*) — "as long as heaven and earth." Ordinary lovers' hyperbole; for a 400-year-old, a literal description and a curse.
- **[海枯石烂](/blog/hai-ku-shi-lan)** (*hǎi kū shí làn*) — "till seas dry and stones crumble." The vow humans make and cannot keep.
- **[刻骨铭心](/blog/ke-gu-ming-xin)** (*kè gǔ míng xīn*) — "carved into bone, inscribed on the heart." Twenty-two times over.
- **[因果报应](/blog/yin-guo-bao-ying)** (*yīn guǒ bào yìng*) — "cause and effect, retribution." The moral engine of the wish-exchange.

## Frequently asked

**What is 归墟?**
The "Return Void" — in classical Chinese cosmography, the bottomless gulf all waters drain into. In the drama, the ghost realm He Simu rules.

**Is He Simu a ghost?**
No. She was born of the former Ghost King and a mortal woman — an evil spirit from birth who has never been human and never died.

**How old is He Simu?**
400 years old, and she has ruled Guixu for about 300 of them.

**Why can't He Simu feel anything?**
She has no senses of her own. She experiences the world through Duan Xu's five senses, via the bond between them.

**What are the 22 graves?**
The twenty-two humans she has loved across four centuries. All of them died — because humans do, and she doesn't.

**How does the wish-exchange work?**
She grants a person's wish and takes their soul in payment. The price is exact.
