**Quick answer:** The Chinese title is **天才，女友** (*Tiāncái, Nǚyǒu*) — **"Genius, Girlfriend"** — and the **comma is deliberate.** It sets two identities side by side without joining them: she is a genius, *and separately* a girlfriend, and the drama's argument is that the world keeps making her pick one. The English "Genius Girlfriend" deletes the comma and, with it, the point. The heroine **林知夏 (Lín Zhīxià)** has a name meaning roughly **"knows summer"**; the hero **江逾白 (Jiāng Yúbái)** has one built on 逾 ("to exceed"). The drama also carried two other titles before broadcast — 《去有你的夏天》 and 《当你耀眼时》 — and both explain something the final title hides.

## The comma

Chinese drama titles almost never use punctuation. When one does, it's doing work.

**天才，女友** is two nouns with a pause between them:

- **天才 (tiāncái)** — genius. 天 "heaven" + 才 "talent": talent that came from the sky rather than from effort.
- **女友 (nǚyǒu)** — girlfriend.

Without the comma it's a compound: *genius girlfriend*, one thing, a girlfriend who happens to be clever. That's the English title, and it's a much smaller idea.

With the comma they're two separate labels applied to one person. And the drama is precisely about the friction between them — a woman whose intelligence makes her socially unreachable, who is called a **怪胎** (*guàitāi*, "freak") for the same quality that makes her exceptional. The comma is the gap she lives in.

There's a second reading available too, and Chinese viewers have made it: as a form of address. *Genius — girlfriend.* Two ways one person is called, by different people, for different reasons.

## 林知夏 (Lín Zhīxià) — the heroine

- **林 (Lín)** — a common surname; as a word, "forest."
- **知 (zhī)** — to know, to understand, to perceive.
- **夏 (xià)** — summer.

**"Knows summer."**

For a character defined by an IQ of 174, naming her with **知** — the character for knowing — is the obvious move. The interesting part is what she knows: not a subject, not a fact. **A season.** Warmth, brightness, the part of the year that is felt rather than solved.

That's the joke and the ache of the character at once. She is named for knowing the one thing she can't reason her way into.

And 夏 is not decoration. The novel's other title is **《去有你的夏天》** — *"to the summer where you are."* The summer in the title *is* the summer in her name, and the phrase points at a person. Her name is half of a sentence the other title completes.

## 江逾白 (Jiāng Yúbái) — the hero

- **江 (Jiāng)** — surname; as a word, "river" (specifically a large one — the Yangtze is 长江).
- **逾 (yú)** — to exceed, to surpass, to go beyond. A relatively literary character.
- **白 (bái)** — white; also "clear," "plain," "bright."

Read straight: **"surpassing white,"** or "beyond white" — whiter than white, clearer than clear.

Names built on 逾 are uncommon and slightly bookish, which suits a character written as the effortless top student. And the pairing with 江 gives a river-and-brightness image that Chinese classical poetry uses constantly for dawn light on water.

Set against her name, the contrast is neat: **she knows summer; he exceeds white.** Hers is warm and seasonal, his is cool and luminous. One is heat, the other is light.

*(You'll see 江宇白 in some English listings. The Chinese sources use 江逾白 — 逾, not 宇.)*

## The two discarded titles

Both earlier titles are more romantic than the one that shipped, and each reveals a different face of the story.

**《去有你的夏天》** — *"To the summer where you are."* Warm, longing, and built around 夏 — her name. This is the title of the love story.

**《当你耀眼时》** — *"When you are dazzling."* 耀眼 (*yàoyǎn*) means dazzling or glaring — light bright enough to be *hard to look at*. That's the sharper of the two: it names her brilliance as something that makes people flinch. This is the title of the isolation story.

The broadcast title, 天才，女友, splits the difference by simply naming the two halves and putting a comma between them.

## Frequently asked

**What is Genius Girlfriend's Chinese name?**
天才，女友 (*Tiāncái, Nǚyǒu*), with a comma.

**Why is there a comma in the title?**
It separates two identities rather than combining them — genius *and* girlfriend, as competing labels for one person.

**What does 林知夏 mean?**
"Knows summer" — 知 (to know) + 夏 (summer), under the surname 林 (forest).

**What does 江逾白 mean?**
Roughly "surpassing white" — 逾 (to exceed) + 白 (white/clear), under the surname 江 (river).

**Is it 江逾白 or 江宇白?**
江逾白. The 宇 spelling appears in some English listings but Chinese sources use 逾.

**What were the earlier titles?**
《去有你的夏天》 ("to the summer where you are") and 《当你耀眼时》 ("when you are dazzling").
