**Quick answer:** **Pang Dong Lai (胖东来, *Pàng Dōng Lái*)** is a supermarket chain in **Xuchang, Henan** that has become the most-discussed employer in China — not for pay alone, but for a package that reads as impossible in a country whose work vocabulary includes **996** and **牛马**.

Staff get **40 days of paid annual leave**, a **7-hour working day**, every store closed on **Tuesdays**, and **10 days of 不开心假** — "unhappy leave," which you may take simply because you don't feel like working, and which **managers are not permitted to refuse**. Average take-home pay ran **¥8,974/month in 2025** and **¥9,598 in early 2026**. Staff mistreated by customers can claim a **委屈奖**, a "grievance award," of up to ¥5,000.

Then the detail that makes the whole story strange: in **February 2026** the company polled its workforce on trading some pay for even more leave. **82.38% said no** — they wanted things left as they are.

For context on the leave figure: a typical Chinese enterprise offers around **five** days of statutory annual leave. Pang Dong Lai offers **forty**.

## 不开心假 — the one everybody talks about

**不开心假** (*bù kāi xīn jià*) breaks down as plainly as it sounds: 不开心 = "not happy," 假 = "leave."

Ten days a year, taken because you are not in the mood. No doctor's note, no explanation, no justification of what you'll do with it.

The crucial part is the enforcement. Founder **Yu Donglai (于东来)** stated publicly that **management cannot decline the request — refusing it is itself a violation.** That inversion is what makes it real rather than decorative. Most "unlimited leave" policies fail because approval stays discretionary and nobody wants to be the person who asks. Here, saying no is the punishable act.

Alongside it sits the **委屈奖** (*wěi qu jiǎng*), the "grievance award." 委屈 is a precise and very Chinese word — the feeling of having been wronged and having to swallow it. Retail staff absorb that daily. Pang Dong Lai pays for it, up to ¥5,000, which quietly reverses the customer-is-always-right premise the entire industry runs on.

## The vote that makes the story interesting

Here is where it stops being a list of perks.

Between **23–24 February 2026** the company surveyed all **10,214** formal contract employees about a proposal to **reduce pay in exchange for more time off**. **9,686** valid responses came back.

**82.38% — 7,979 people — voted to keep things exactly as they are.** And **97.55%** said they were satisfied or very satisfied with the existing 40-day arrangement.

Read that carefully, because it cuts against the easy narrative. This is not a workforce being handed leave it begged for. It is a workforce being *offered more* and answering: **we're good.** Forty days and a 7-hour day is apparently the point at which Chinese retail workers stop wanting time and start wanting the money.

## Why this is the anti-内卷 company

To understand why Chinese people talk about this supermarket the way they do, you need the vocabulary it's a rebuttal to.

- **[内卷](/slang/nei-juan)** (*nèi juǎn*), "involution" — competition that increases effort without increasing reward. Everyone works harder; nobody gets further ahead.
- **[996](/slang/996)** — 9am to 9pm, six days a week. The tech-industry schedule that became a national argument.
- **[牛马](/slang/niu-ma)** (*niú mǎ*), "cattle and horses" — beasts of burden. What workers call **themselves**: labour without personhood.
- **[打工人](/slang/da-gong-ren)** (*dǎ gōng rén*), "wage worker" — the ironic, weary self-label of the ordinary employee.

And the exits people chose when the system wouldn't change:

- **[躺平](/slang/tang-ping)** (*tǎng píng*), "lying flat" — opting out, doing the minimum. ([Full explainer →](/blog/tang-ping-lying-flat-chinese-millennial-movement-meaning))
- **[摆烂](/slang/bai-lan)** (*bǎi làn*), "letting it rot" — giving up on improving a situation on purpose.

躺平 and 摆烂 are what you get when 内卷 has no exit. Pang Dong Lai's significance is that it proposes a **third option**: an employer that simply pays well and stops early, and remains extremely profitable while doing it. That's why it functions less as a company in Chinese discourse than as **an argument** — evidence that the 996/牛马 bargain is a choice rather than an economic law.

## The economics, because the perks aren't charity

The obvious objection is that this only works if you're losing money on purpose. Pang Dong Lai isn't.

**Yu Donglai** is a middle-school dropout who started with a tobacco shop in **1990** and a small retail store in **1995**. By 2024 the business was doing close to **¥17 billion** in annual sales.

The model has some deliberate levers:

- **Capped margins.** Profit on fast-moving consumer goods is limited to around **30%**, and the "backstage fees" suppliers normally pay for shelf placement are eliminated.
- **Own-brand depth.** Roughly **30%** of revenue comes from its own **DL** label, developed directly with factories and farms, cutting wholesalers out.
- **Trust as the product.** In **June 2024**, after discovering sanitation problems at a supplier, the company **paid the whistleblower ¥100,000** and refunded **¥1,000 to every affected customer** — a response so far outside Chinese retail norms that it became national news. In a market with chronic distrust of food safety, that reputation is the moat.

The staff policy isn't separate from this. Retail quality is delivered by people, and rested, well-paid, non-desperate people deliver it better. The perks *are* the strategy.

## The part that complicates the halo

Two things are worth knowing before treating this as a template.

**It's one man's company.** In **August 2026**, Yu Donglai announced on a livestream that the Xuchang Life Plaza store — open **24 years**, with nearly **¥1 billion** in first-half sales, a projected **¥2 billion** for the year and **over ¥100 million** in annual profit — would **close at the end of the year**. A decision like that, taken at peak profitability and announced on a livestream, tells you the model is inseparable from the founder's personal judgement. That is not a governance structure anyone can copy.

**It's one city.** Pang Dong Lai operates in Xuchang, a mid-sized Henan city, with local wage levels and local rents. Whether the same arithmetic survives in Shanghai or Shenzhen is genuinely unproven — and the model's admirers and critics have been arguing about exactly that for two years.

None of which makes the 82% vote less remarkable. It just means the honest reading is: *this is a real counterexample, not a blueprint.*
